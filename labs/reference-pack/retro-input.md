# Anonymised retrospective input — Tempo 2.0, Sprint 1 (reference pack)

Raw, **anonymised** feedback gathered from the team before the Sprint 1
retrospective. Names have been removed on purpose — this is exactly how retro
input should reach an AI. You feed this to AI in **Lab 12** to theme it and turn
it into improvement actions. Handle it as sensitive: keep it inside the team, use
it to find patterns, never to judge individuals. Fictional.

Prompt the team was given: *"What should we Start, Stop or Continue — and how did
the sprint feel?"*

---

- The design dependency hurt us — when Ken went on leave, two stories stalled with no one to answer design questions.
- We committed 34 points knowing a designer would be away for 3 days. That was optimistic.
- The midnight streak-reset fix went really smoothly — clear bug, good tests, done early. More of that.
- The Android reminder bug was a rabbit hole. We didn't spike it before committing, so the estimate was fiction.
- Priya was quick to answer product questions, which unblocked the combined-streak logic fast. Keep that.
- The legal review of the third-party push service should have been started before the sprint, not during it.
- Stand-ups were useful but sometimes ran long when we debugged live instead of taking it offline.
- I didn't know the shared-habit edge cases until day 4; getting those clarified up front would have saved rework.
- We had no design backup plan. One person being away shouldn't stop two stories.
- Pairing on the combined-streak logic worked well — fewer bugs, faster review.
- We keep discovering dependencies mid-sprint (design, legal, third-party). We should surface them in planning.
- Testing the Android workaround across devices at the end was a scramble. Spread QA through the sprint.
- Morale was actually good despite the slip — the shared-habit feature is exciting to build.
- The board wasn't always up to date, so the burndown looked worse than reality on a couple of days.
- Estimates for anything touching the OS (notifications) are unreliable; we should spike unknowns first.
